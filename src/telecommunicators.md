When an emergency happens, every second matters. The Raleigh County 911 Center serves as the vital link between the public and the emergency responders who protect our community. Public Safety Telecommunicators are the first — First Responders.

Our highly trained Public Safety Telecommunicators use specialized technology, established procedures, and coordinated communications to ensure emergency calls are handled efficiently and the appropriate resources are dispatched.

## 1. What happens when you call 911?

When you dial 911, your call is routed to our Communications Center and answered by a trained Public Safety Telecommunicator.

The telecommunicator will ask questions to determine:

- Where the emergency is occurring.
- What type of emergency is taking place.
- Whether anyone is injured or in immediate danger.
- What additional information responders need.

These questions are important. While some information may seem repetitive, each question helps us understand the situation and coordinate the appropriate response.

**Remember: Emergency responders are often dispatched while the telecommunicator continues gathering information.**

## 2. The technology behind the response

Modern emergency communications rely on specialized technology that assists Telecommunicators in processing calls and coordinating emergency resources.

Our Communications Center utilizes several systems that work together to support emergency operations:

- **Computer-Aided Dispatch (CAD):** Allows telecommunicators to enter incident information, document important details, and coordinate the dispatch of emergency personnel.
- **Emergency Telephone Systems:** Help route incoming emergency calls to the appropriate communications center and provide available caller and location information.
- **Mapping and Location Technology:** Assists telecommunicators in identifying emergency locations and helping responders navigate to incidents.
- **Radio Communications:** Provides the ability to communicate directly with law enforcement, fire, and emergency medical personnel in the field.
- **Emergency Medical Dispatch (EMD):** Provides structured protocols that help trained telecommunicators deliver appropriate medical instructions to callers while emergency responders are enroute.

Technology is an important part of emergency communications, but it is the training, judgment, and professionalism of our telecommunicators that bring these systems together.

## 3. Helping you while help is on the way

Our responsibility does not end when emergency responders are dispatched.

When appropriate, telecommunicators remain on the telephone with callers and provide instructions based on established protocols.

This may include:

- Providing CPR instructions.
- Assisting with choking emergencies.
- Providing instructions for controlling severe bleeding.
- Giving important safety instructions.
- Gathering updates about the patient's condition.
- Relaying additional information to responding personnel.

Through Emergency Medical Dispatch protocols, telecommunicators can help callers take important lifesaving actions before emergency personnel arrive.

**Your cooperation and ability to follow instructions can make a difference.**

## 4. Things to remember when calling 911

Knowing what to do when calling 911 can help us get assistance to you more efficiently.

### Know your location

Try to provide the exact street address, road name, business name, or nearest identifiable landmark. Never assume the telecommunicator automatically knows where you are.

### Answer all questions

Listen carefully and answer each question as accurately as possible. The information helps determine the appropriate response.

### Stay on the phone

Do not hang up until the Telecommunicator tells you it is okay. Responders may already be on the way, but additional information or instructions may still be needed.

### Follow instructions

Telecommunicators may provide medical or safety instructions while you wait for responders. Follow those instructions to the best of your ability.

### Remain calm

We understand emergencies are stressful. Try to speak clearly, listen carefully, and provide the information requested.

## 5. A team effort, 24 hours a day

Behind every emergency response is a team of dedicated professionals working around the clock.

Our telecommunicators serve as the critical connection between the public and the law enforcement, fire, and emergency medical personnel responding to calls for assistance.

Whether answering an emergency call, coordinating multiple agencies, or providing instructions during a medical emergency, our employees remain committed to serving the residents and visitors of Raleigh County.

**We may not be the faces you see arriving at an emergency, but we are there with you from the very beginning of the call.**
